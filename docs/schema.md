# Database schema

Field dictionary for the canonical, POS-agnostic schema in `supabase/migrations/`. Everything here is derived from the migrations; when they change, this file changes with them. Nothing in this document is specific to one shop: identifiers, keys and payloads are illustrative shapes only.

Two schemas exist:

- `public` holds every table, enum, view and query function and is exposed through the Data API to the `authenticated` and `service_role` roles. `anon` holds no privilege on it.
- `app` holds helper functions used by row-level-security policies and triggers, the fact-reading functions behind the analytics views, the analytics refresh, and the incremental sync schedule (its settings row, its dispatch tickets and their functions). It is not exposed through the API; `authenticated` may execute the helpers it needs, `service_role` alone may run the refresh, and only `postgres` (the role of the jobs, the Edge Function and `pg_cron`) reaches the schedule. The notification dispatcher's tables (link codes, bot polling state, its settings row and its tickets) and its functions live there too, reachable by `postgres` only (see "Notifications and alerts (phase 12)").

## Conventions

- Every table has a `uuid` primary key `id` generated by `gen_random_uuid()`, except `tenant_members` (composite key) and `pos_account_tokens` (keyed by the account).
- Every row that belongs to a tenant carries `tenant_id`. Rows that belong to a store also carry `store_id`; rows that come from one POS account carry `pos_account_id`.
- Identifiers assigned by a POS are kept verbatim in `external_id` columns (`text`). Foreign relations to POS entities are expressed by external id, never by our uuid, so a re-synced catalogue never breaks facts.
- Timestamps are `timestamptz`. Calendar days are `date`. Money is `numeric(14,2)` in the tenant's currency. Quantities are `numeric(12,3)`. Percentages (`_pct` columns) are `numeric(6,3)` where `1.100` means 1.1 %. Rates and shares in the aggregate tables and query functions are fractions where `0.250` means 25 %.
- Master-data tables carry `active`, `first_seen_at` and `last_seen_at`, maintained by the sync: a row disappearing from the POS catalogue is marked inactive, never deleted.
- Manual-input tables carry `created_by` (defaults to `auth.uid()`, frozen by a trigger on update), `created_at` and `updated_at` (set by a trigger).
- No table is ever written by the browser except the manual-input tables, `strategy_reports`, the two legal ledgers `consent_records` and `data_subject_requests` (a `notifications` consent excepted), and the notification settings `alert_rules` and `channel_policies` (owners with an unlimited scope only); every other write comes from the sync jobs and the messaging jobs running as `service_role`. The aggregate tables (`agg_*`) are written only by `app.refresh_analytics()`, which the sync jobs call and which an owner or manager may trigger through `refresh_my_analytics()`.

`strategy_source` says how a plan reached the database: `skill` when the local strategy skill published it, `routine` when a scheduled cloud routine did, `pasted` when a person pasted it in the web app.

`consent_purpose` `notifications` (added by its own migration, `notify_consent_purpose`, so the label commits before any row uses it) covers the daily digests and operational alerts a member receives; it is separate from `staff_ops`, so withdrawing one never stops the other. `channel_kind` lists every place a message can go; only `zalo_bot`, `email`, `telegram` and `webhook` have an adapter in phase 12 (`app.channel_kind_available`), the other kinds exist so their policies are data. `message_class` separates internal operational messages, which are not advertising (Decree 91/2020 Art. 3(1)), from the customer classes of phases 17 to 19.

`legal_form` records the owner's entity type under the personal-data law: a household business or micro-enterprise is exempt from the cross-border transfer dossier, a small enterprise may defer it, an enterprise must file it. `unknown` is the default until the owner confirms. `e_invoice_status` says whether the store's POS issues cash-register e-invoices. `tran_no_reset_period` says how often the POS restarts its receipt numbering, which is why receipt claims are keyed on store, business date and receipt number together.

| `last_seen_at` | timestamptz | no | Last sync that saw the store |

`opening_hours` is an object keyed by weekday `mon`, `tue`, `wed`, `thu`, `fri`, `sat`, `sun`. Each value is a list of `[open, close]` pairs in local `HH:MM`, so a split day is two pairs and a closed day is an empty list or a missing key:

```json
{"mon": [["07:00", "22:30"]], "sat": [["07:00", "12:00"], ["16:00", "23:00"]]}
```

The check constraint `stores_opening_hours_valid` (`app.opening_hours_valid`) refuses any other shape: the value is null or such an object, its keys are those seven weekdays, each value is a list, and each pair holds two times `H:MM` or `HH:MM` from `0:00` to `23:59`, or `24:00`. An object without a single pair (`{}`, empty lists) is allowed and counts as unknown hours for the schedule. `authenticated` and `service_role` may execute the two validators, so a future writer of stores passes the constraint; the trigger functions that set `sync_resumed_at` (`app.mark_store_sync_resumed`, `app.mark_account_sync_resumed`) are `security definer`.

### `sale_payloads`

The POS payloads of the sales of one store and business date, kept here instead of in `invoices.raw`, `invoice_lines.raw` and `invoice_payments.raw` (see the storage rule). Primary key (`store_id`, `business_date`); deleted with its store or tenant.

| Column | Type | Null | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tenant_id` | uuid | no | Tenant |
| `store_id` | uuid | no | Store |
| `business_date` | date | no | Business date of the sales, as on `invoices` |
| `payload` | text | no | One JSON record per sale, records separated by line breaks and ordered by `external_id`, compressed with lz4: `external_id` (the invoice's), `invoice` (the sale payload), `lines` (the payload of each invoice line keyed by its `external_id`) and `payments` (the payload of each tender keyed by its `sequence_no`) |

## Consent and legal records

These tables implement the duties of the personal-data law and the anti-spam decree described in ADR-021: a per-purpose consent ledger with evidence and a working opt-out, data-subject requests tracked against their statutory deadlines, and an outbound-message log kept at least one year.

### `consent_records`

Append-only ledger of consent. One row per grant; a revocation sets `revoked_at` on the row instead of deleting it, so the evidence of both events survives. At most one active row (`revoked_at` null) exists per (`tenant_id`, `subject_type`, `subject_id`, `purpose`), enforced by the partial unique index `consent_records_one_active_per_subject_purpose_key`; a new grant after a revocation is a new row.

#### Append-only rule

The trigger `consent_records_keep_grant` (`app.keep_consent_grant()`, before update) enforces the ledger for every role, the service role included, because it is a data rule rather than an access rule. Every column except `revoked_at` is frozen once the row exists: an update that touches `tenant_id`, `subject_type`, `subject_id`, `purpose`, `policy_version`, `channel`, `granted_at`, `evidence` or `created_at` fails with `consent_records is an append-only ledger: only revoked_at may change`. `revoked_at` may go from null to a timestamp once; clearing it or moving it fails with `consent_records revocation is final`, and a value before `granted_at` fails with `consent_records revocation cannot precede the grant`. `authenticated` holds no `DELETE` privilege on the table, so a wrong grant is corrected by revoking it and recording a new one.

#### Deadline rule

`app.dsr_deadlines(received, country_code, time_zone, uses_processor)` returns the two deadlines:

- `first_response_due_at` is `received` plus two working days. A working day is Monday to Friday in `time_zone` that is not a global `holidays` row (`tenant_id` null) of `country_code`; tenant-specific custom holidays do not count. Counting starts on the day after receipt and keeps the time of day, so a request received Friday 10:00 is due Tuesday 10:00, and one received on 29 April is due 5 May because 30 April, 1 May and the weekend are skipped. The function scans the 120 days after receipt and raises an exception if it cannot find two working days in that window, so the deadline is never silently null.
- `completion_due_at` is `received` plus 20 calendar days, or 30 when `uses_processor` is true.

The trigger `data_subject_requests_fill_deadlines` runs before insert, reads the tenant's `country_code` and `timezone`, and fills whichever of the two due columns was left null. Values given explicitly are kept.

## Analytics

Roadmap phase 8 (ADR-010, ADR-026). Aggregates are ordinary tables refreshed by a function, protected by the same store-scoped RLS as the fact tables, and read by `security invoker` query functions so that RLS always applies to the caller. The metric definitions that these objects implement are in `docs/metrics.md`.

### Hot and cold rule

Every store-day with `business_date` **before yesterday** is read from the aggregate tables; **yesterday and later** are computed live from the fact tables. `app.analytics_live_from()` returns the cut-over date (`current_date - 1`, evaluated in the database's UTC clock, which always covers the local "yesterday" of a UTC+7 store). The query functions apply the rule through `app.daily_sales()` and `app.hourly_sales()`, so today is correct after every incremental sync without a refresh, and yesterday is correct even before the nightly refresh has run. The live half passes the requested `store_ids` down to `app.sales_invoices()`, which reads `invoices` through the `(store_id, business_date)` index, so a call costs the requested stores' invoices of the live days and never a scan over other tenants. Since migration `live_half_lateral`, `app.daily_sales_facts()` reaches each invoice's lines and cash tenders with lateral lookups on `invoice_lines (invoice_id)` and `invoice_payments (invoice_id)` instead of hash joins over both tables; the signature, the columns and their expressions are unchanged. On a local two-store tenant with two years of history, two live days of both stores take about 2.5 ms instead of 49 ms, a 93-day refresh-sized read about 68 ms instead of 86 ms, and a full-history `app.daily_sales()` about 3 ms instead of 50 ms; `app.hourly_sales_facts()` keeps its joins (about 1.5 ms). Item, category, payment, shift, size, modifier, attach and basket aggregates have no live counterpart: they appear for a day once that day has been refreshed (the incremental sync refreshes the days it touched; the nightly job refreshes the trailing window).

The cut-over date is a freshness rule; the **as-of day** of month-to-date figures and forecasts is a separate, store-local rule. `app.analytics_as_of(store_ids, p_at default now())` is the earliest, over the given stores, of the store-local business date at `p_at` (store timezone and `business_day_start_minutes`) minus one day: the last complete business day. Between 00:00 and 07:00 in Vietnam the UTC date still names the local previous day, so a UTC rule would drop the completed local yesterday from month to date; the store-local rule does not, and that day is served live because it is never before the cut-over date of a UTC+7 store. With no visible store it falls back to the UTC date of `p_at` minus one.

Sales columns count non-voided invoices only; `voided_invoices`, `voided_amount` and `edited_invoices` come from `invoices.status`.

Primary key `(store_id, business_date, item_type_external_id)`, the text `uncategorized` standing in for a null type. `role` is the `item_type_roles` mapping of the type for the store's POS account, default `other`. Columns `item_type_name`, `quantity`, `net_amount`.

#### `agg_payment_daily`

Primary key `(store_id, business_date, payment_method_external_id)`, the text `unknown` standing in for a null method. `business_date` is the **invoice's** business date, not `invoice_payments.business_date`: a tender is attributed to its invoice, so Σ `amount` of a store-day equals that day's `agg_daily_sales.paid_amount` by construction, even when a resync has left a tender dated differently from its invoice. `payment_method_name` is the method's `payment_methods.name`, else the `invoice_payments.payment_method_name` of its tenders (the greatest when they differ), and null when neither names it. `kind` comes from `payment_methods`, default `other`. `invoices` is the distinct invoices paid with the method, `amount` the Σ tendered, `fee_amount` the fee the POS reported, `fee_estimate_amount` the Σ of `amount × fee_pct / 100 + fixed_fee_amount` using the latest `payment_fee_rates` row effective on the invoice's business date, store-specific before tenant-wide.

#### `agg_shift_cash`

Primary key `(store_id, shift_id)`, `shift_id` referencing `shifts` with cascade delete. `business_date` is the business date of `opened_at` under the store's day-start rule. `orders`, `sales_amount`, `opening_float_amount`, `expected_cash_amount`, `drawer_amount` and `variance_amount` (= drawer − expected) are copied from the shift row; `cash_sales_amount` is recomputed as the cash tenders of the shift's non-voided invoices. Also `shift_external_id`, `cashier_name`, `opened_at`, `closed_at`. Because the key is the shift and not the date, the refresh deletes both the rows dated in its range and the rows of every shift whose business date now falls in its range, so a shift whose `opened_at` was corrected, or whose store changed timezone or day start, moves to its new date instead of colliding with its old row.

#### `agg_size_mix_daily`

Primary key `(store_id, business_date, base_name, size_label)`; item lines with a `size_label` only, `base_name` falling back to the item name. Columns `quantity`, `net_amount`.

#### `agg_modifier_prefs_daily`

Primary key `(store_id, business_date, modifier_name)`; `count` is the number of modifier lines of non-voided invoices carrying that name.

#### `agg_basket_pairs_28d`

Primary key `(store_id, as_of_date, item_a_external_id, item_b_external_id)` with `item_a_external_id < item_b_external_id`; an item id falls back to the item name when the POS sent none, as in `agg_item_daily`. Over the 28 days ending `as_of_date`, item lines of non-voided invoices, pairs within one invoice, at least 3 `pair_invoices`. `item_a_name` and `item_b_name` are the `invoice_lines.item_name` of each item in that window (the greatest when it sold under several names). `support_a` and `support_b` are the fractions of the window's invoices (those with at least one item line) containing each item; `lift` = (pair share) ÷ (`support_a` × `support_b`). Modifiers never appear. Each refresh rewrites the rows for `as_of_date = p_to`.

#### `agg_attach_daily`

Primary key `(store_id, business_date)`. Roles come from `item_type_roles`. `drink_invoices` are invoices with at least one item line of role `drink`; `topping_invoices` and `snack_invoices` those that also contain a `topping` or `snack` line; `topping_lines` and `snack_lines` count those lines on drink invoices; `attach_rate_topping` = `topping_invoices` ÷ `drink_invoices` and `attach_rate_snack` likewise. A rate is null when the day had no drink invoice, and also when no `item_type_roles` row of the store's POS account carries that role: an unmapped role is unmeasured, not zero. Once the role is mapped, a day without such a line reads 0. Summing numerators and denominators over the measured days of a period gives the period rate.

### `analytics_refreshes`

One row per `app.refresh_analytics()` call: `tenant_id`, `from_date`, `to_date`, `started_at`, `finished_at`, `rows` (rows written across every aggregate table). Readable by the data roles of the tenant.

### Refresh

`app.refresh_analytics(p_tenant uuid, p_from date, p_to date) returns bigint` is `security definer` and executable by `service_role` only. It first takes the transaction-level advisory lock `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('refresh_analytics'), hashtext(p_tenant::text))`, so overlapping refreshes of one tenant (the nightly job, an incremental sync, a manager's button) queue behind each other instead of racing on delete-then-insert, while refreshes of different tenants run in parallel. Then, in the same transaction, it logs the start, deletes every aggregate row of the tenant in the range (`agg_basket_pairs_28d` for `as_of_date = p_to`; `agg_shift_cash` also by shift, see above), re-inserts them from the fact tables, refreshes the basket pairs for `p_to`, and closes the log row with the row count. A rolled-back refresh leaves no log row. Since migration `refresh_overrides_and_catch_up` it reads `tenants.analytics_inputs_version` right after the lock; in `agg_category_daily` and in the role lines that feed `agg_attach_daily` a line takes the type of the `item_category_overrides` row of its invoice's POS account and item key (the type's name from `item_types`), else its POS type, while `agg_item_daily` keeps the POS type because the query-time functions apply overrides themselves; after the last insert it calls `app.shrink_analytics_pending(p_tenant, p_from, p_to, version)`. Without overrides it writes exactly what the previous body wrote (pgTAP on the seed, and every aggregate row of the local tenant over 14 days), and its timing did not move (3 days about 0.43 s, 93 days about 0.79 s on the local tenant). The seed calls it for the demo tenant, so `supabase db reset` leaves the aggregates populated. On the seed (two stores, seven days, about 350 invoices) a refresh takes about 30 ms; on a synthetic tenant of two stores with about 54,000 invoices over 91 days a 92-day refresh takes about 1.3 s, far under the two-minute acceptance limit of phase 8, and `month_pace` and `forecast_accuracy` answer in under 0.1 s.

`public.refresh_my_analytics(p_from date, p_to date, p_tenant uuid default null) returns bigint` is the browser-callable wrapper for owners and managers. It rejects a range longer than 92 days, rejects `p_tenant` when the caller is not an owner or manager of it, and with a null `p_tenant` refreshes every tenant where the caller holds one of those roles, in tenant id order so two such calls take the tenant locks in the same order (raising `insufficient_privilege` when there is none). Staff, viewers, publishers and `anon` cannot refresh.

### Views

`v_recent_daily_sales` and `v_recent_hourly_sales` (`security_invoker = true`) select from `app.daily_sales_facts(null, current_date - 45, current_date + 1)` and `app.hourly_sales_facts(…)`, which compute the same columns as `agg_daily_sales` and `agg_hourly_sales` directly from `invoices`, `invoice_lines`, `invoice_payments` and `payment_methods`. Because the views run as the caller, RLS on the fact tables decides what they return; `anon` cannot select from them. They are the reconciliation tool for **every store the caller can see**: a filter on `store_id` applied to the view is not pushed into the function, so each select computes the 46 days for all visible stores (and, for `service_role`, every tenant). Reports read the query functions, which take `store_ids`; the views are for the app's own checks and for comparing live figures with the aggregates.

### Publisher and aggregates

The `publisher` role reads zero rows of every aggregate table, the refresh log and the live views, exactly as it reads zero rows of the fact tables: aggregates reveal revenue, and ADR-013 wants a leaked publisher credential to read no sale. The AI data pack of phase 11 is therefore produced by an owner or manager session (or by the `fnb` CLI with the service role), and the publisher only writes the resulting plan into `strategy_reports`.

## Notifications and alerts (phase 12)

Roadmap phase 12. Digests and alerts reach the owner and managers through one Postgres outbox: `outbound_messages` is the queue and the one-year log, every send decision is taken in SQL when a dispatcher claims a message, and the channel credentials stay outside the database (rows name an environment variable, never its value). ### `alert_events`

Alert episodes: one open row per (`tenant_id`, `dedupe_key`) (partial unique index `alert_events_one_open_key`), with `rule_key`, the subject (`store_id`, `pos_account_id`, `channel_id`, each a composite foreign key to its tenant's row with cascade), `business_date`, `severity`, `state` (`open` or `resolved`, with `resolved_at` and `resolution` set together), `opened_at`, `last_seen_at`, `occurrences`, `folded_occurrences`, `last_notified_at` (the stored cool-down), `notifications`, `suppressed_reason` (`cool_down`, `no_destination`, `root_cause_open`), `source` and count-only `facts` (`app.facts_are_count_only`). Episodes are opened by `app.raise_alert` and resolved by `app.reconcile_alerts` (see "Episodes and alerts" below).

### `digests`

One daily digest per (`tenant_id`, `business_date`, `scope_key`), where `scope_key` is the md5 of the scope's sorted store ids: `store_ids`, `all_stores`, `state` (`built` with its model, version, fingerprint, trigger, data state and `built_at`, or `failed` with `attempts` and `next_attempt_at`), `revision` (0, or 1 after one correction), `built_as`, `revised_at`, `last_error_code`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. The model holds aggregates and store, item and shift names only. Digests are found due by `app.due_digests` and stored by `app.complete_digest` and `app.fail_digest` (see "Digest functions" below).

### `outbound_messages` as the outbox

Phase 12 made `sent_at` nullable without a default and added `created_at`, `kind` (`digest`, `digest_correction`, `alert`, `alert_reminder`, `alert_resolved`, `test`, `link_code`, `link_notice`, `link_confirmation`, or null for log rows), `message_class`, `channel_id`, `store_id`, `digest_id` (composite foreign keys with `on delete set null` of the column), `alert_event_id` and `resend_of` (`on delete set null`), `business_date`, `severity`, `dedupe_key` (unique per tenant), `params` (count-only), `title` (at most 200) and `body` (at most 8,000), `body_sha256`, `decision`, `hold_reason`, `not_before`, `expires_at`, `attempts` (0 to 10), `claimed_at`, `lease_until`, `send_started_at`, `last_error_code`. A queue row needs a class and a dedupe key, and a destination while it waits; a `sending` row needs its claim and lease, a `sent` or `delivered` row its `sent_at`. For a queue row `channel` is the destination's kind, `recipient_ref` is `channel:<id>` (never an address), the subject is the member, `purpose` the policy's, and `template_ref` names the template (`alert.<rule>.v1`, `test.v1`, `link_notice.v1`, …).

The trigger `outbound_messages_guard` (`app.guard_outbound_message()`, before update) enforces the state machine on rows with a kind and raises `55000` on anything else:

```
queued  -> held | sending | cancelled
held    -> queued | sending | cancelled
sending -> sent | queued | failed | uncertain | cancelled (only once the destination vanished)
sent    -> delivered | failed
uncertain -> sent | failed
```

`id`, `tenant_id`, `kind` and `created_at` never change. A row in `sent`, `delivered`, `failed`, `cancelled` or `uncertain` is frozen except: `title`, `body`, `params`, `dedupe_key` and `subject_id` may become null (body purge, redaction), `recipient_ref` may become `redacted`, the foreign keys (`channel_id`, `store_id`, `digest_id`, `alert_event_id`, `consent_record_id`, `resend_of`) may become null when their row is deleted, and `status`, `sent_at`, `provider_message_id` and `last_error_code` change only with an allowed transition.

Indexes: the partial unique dedupe key, due messages by `coalesce(not_before, created_at)` while `queued` or `held`, leases while `sending`, per-destination activity by `coalesce(sent_at, claimed_at)` for the caps, (`tenant_id`, `created_at desc`), and the digest, episode and resend references.

The read policy `outbound_messages_select_for_managers_or_recipient` replaced the phase 2 one: owners and managers with an unlimited scope read every row of the tenant, a store-limited owner or manager reads the rows of their stores, and every member reads the messages to their own destinations (`app.current_user_channel_ids()`).

### Episodes and alerts

## Row-level security

Every table in `public` has RLS enabled. `anon` holds no privilege on any table. `authenticated` holds `SELECT` on every table except `pos_account_tokens`, `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `DELETE` only on the manual-input tables (`item_costs`, `payment_fee_rates`, `item_type_roles`, `holidays`, `targets`), the report settings and mappings (`report_settings`, `item_category_overrides`) and `strategy_reports`, `INSERT`, `UPDATE` without `DELETE` on the legal ledgers `consent_records` and `data_subject_requests`, and `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `DELETE` on the notification settings `alert_rules` and `channel_policies`. On `notification_channels` it holds column-level `SELECT` only, without `address`, `secret_name` and `config`. Default privileges are set so that a future table created by the migration role starts read-only for `authenticated` and invisible to `anon`. The sync jobs run as `service_role`, which bypasses RLS.

### Store scope

`tenant_members.store_ids` limits a member to some stores of the tenant. A null scope means every store. Store-scoped fact tables filter on `store_id`; `sync_runs` filters on the stores a run covered (see its section); tenant-wide catalogue tables filter on `tenant_id`; tables whose `store_id` may be null (`item_costs`, `payment_fee_rates`, `strategy_reports`) show tenant-wide rows to every member of the tenant and store rows only to members who can see that store.

## Business date

`invoices.business_date` is assigned by the sync, never by the database, with this rule:

```
local_time     = sold_at at time zone stores.timezone
business_date  = (local_time - make_interval(mins => stores.business_day_start_minutes))::date
```

A store with `business_day_start_minutes = 300` (05:00) books a sale at 00:30 local time to the previous calendar day; with the default 0 the business date is the local calendar day. Store timezones are IANA names, so a zone with daylight saving is handled by Postgres. `invoice_payments.business_date` copies the invoice value. Shifts keep their raw timestamps; reports attribute a shift to the business date of its `opened_at` using the same rule.

## Storage rule for `raw`

Storage on the free tier is finite, and a payload stored on its own row is expensive: an iPOS line carries about a hundred fields, about 3 kB as `jsonb`, and a row under 2 kB is never compressed. Kept that way, the payloads took about two thirds of the database after the full backfill of both shops, well over the 500 MB plan. The sync therefore keeps every payload, but not on the sales rows:

- The sync writes null to `invoices.raw`, `invoice_lines.raw` and `invoice_payments.raw`. The payloads of a store-day's sales go into its `sale_payloads` document, which compresses about ten times, because the sales of a day repeat the same field names and many of the same values.
- The `invoice` part of a record is the full payload the POS returned for the sale, so any future column can be back-filled without a re-sync.
- A line or tender payload leaves out the fields whose value equals the invoice payload's (store, receipt number, timestamps, cashier, customer on iPOS). Read a field missing from it from the invoice payload; every other field is kept as returned.
- `shifts.raw`, `invoice_events.raw` and master data `raw` keep the payload of that entity as returned, on the row.

With this rule the same history fits under the 300 MB ceiling. This is a sync-side rule: the database accepts any JSON. `mapSale` drops the fields shared with the invoice (`packages/sync/test/unit/payloads.test.ts`), and the Postgres repository writes the records through `app.store_sale_payloads` (`supabase/tests/19_sale_payloads.sql`). The in-memory repository keeps the payloads on its rows. Migration `20261108062820_sale_payloads.sql` moves payloads already on the rows into `sale_payloads`; a database that held sales before it returns the space after `vacuum (full, analyze) public.invoice_lines, public.invoice_payments, public.invoices;`. The seed writes the demo tenant in the same shape: its invoices, lines and payments have null `raw` and each of its store-days has a document, so a fresh reset in CI matches production.

## Tenant-consistent composite foreign keys

A row that carries both `tenant_id` and a reference to a child of the tenant (a store or a POS account) references the pair, not the child's id alone:

`stores` and `pos_accounts` declare `unique (tenant_id, id)`; every store-scoped table declares `foreign key (tenant_id, store_id) references stores (tenant_id, id)` and every account-scoped table declares `foreign key (tenant_id, pos_account_id) references pos_accounts (tenant_id, id)`.

The database therefore refuses a row that names tenant A together with a store of tenant B, even when written by the service role or by a bug in a policy. Tables that follow the pattern: `stores`, every catalogue table, `store_items`, `devices`, `invoices`, `invoice_lines`, `invoice_payments`, `invoice_events`, `sale_payloads`, `shifts`, `sync_runs`, `sync_checks`, `item_costs`, `payment_fee_rates`, `item_type_roles`, `targets`, `strategy_reports`, every `agg_*` aggregate table, `alert_rules`, `alert_events` (store, account and destination), `notification_channels` (its member, as (`tenant_id`, `user_id`) to `tenant_members`) and `outbound_messages` (destination, store and digest, each `on delete set null` of the column only). `invoice_events.invoice_id` references `invoices.id` alone with `on delete set null`, because the event must outlive the invoice; its `store_id` and `invoice_external_id` keep it attributable. The legal tables carry `tenant_id` and no store; `consent_records.subject_id` and `data_subject_requests.subject_id` deliberately have no foreign key (see the consent model), and `outbound_messages.consent_record_id` references `consent_records.id` alone with `on delete set null`.
