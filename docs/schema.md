# Database schema

Field dictionary for the canonical, POS-agnostic schema in `supabase/migrations/`. Everything here is derived from the migrations; when they change, this file changes with them. Nothing in this document is specific to one shop: identifiers, keys and payloads are illustrative shapes only.

Two schemas exist:

- `public` holds every table, enum, view and query function and is exposed through the Data API to the `authenticated` and `service_role` roles. `anon` holds no privilege on it.
- `app` holds helper functions used by row-level-security policies and triggers, the fact-reading functions behind the analytics views, and the analytics refresh. It is not exposed through the API; `authenticated` may execute the helpers it needs, `service_role` alone may run the refresh.

## Conventions

- Every table has a `uuid` primary key `id` generated by `gen_random_uuid()`, except `tenant_members` (composite key) and `pos_account_tokens` (keyed by the account).
- Every row that belongs to a tenant carries `tenant_id`. Rows that belong to a store also carry `store_id`; rows that come from one POS account carry `pos_account_id`.
- Identifiers assigned by a POS are kept verbatim in `external_id` columns (`text`). Foreign relations to POS entities are expressed by external id, never by our uuid, so a re-synced catalogue never breaks facts.
- Timestamps are `timestamptz`. Calendar days are `date`. Money is `numeric(14,2)` in the tenant's currency. Quantities are `numeric(12,3)`. Percentages (`_pct` columns) are `numeric(6,3)` where `1.100` means 1.1 %. Rates and shares in the aggregate tables and query functions are fractions where `0.250` means 25 %.
- Master-data tables carry `active`, `first_seen_at` and `last_seen_at`, maintained by the sync: a row disappearing from the POS catalogue is marked inactive, never deleted.
- Manual-input tables carry `created_by` (defaults to `auth.uid()`, frozen by a trigger on update), `created_at` and `updated_at` (set by a trigger).
- No table is ever written by the browser except the manual-input tables, `strategy_reports` and the two legal ledgers `consent_records` and `data_subject_requests`; every other write comes from the sync jobs and the messaging jobs running as `service_role`. The aggregate tables (`agg_*`) are written only by `app.refresh_analytics()`, which the sync jobs call and which an owner or manager may trigger through `refresh_my_analytics()`.

`strategy_source` says how a plan reached the database: `skill` when the local strategy skill published it, `routine` when a scheduled cloud routine did, `pasted` when a person pasted it in the web app.

`legal_form` records the owner's entity type under the personal-data law: a household business or micro-enterprise is exempt from the cross-border transfer dossier, a small enterprise may defer it, an enterprise must file it. `unknown` is the default until the owner confirms. `e_invoice_status` says whether the store's POS issues cash-register e-invoices. `tran_no_reset_period` says how often the POS restarts its receipt numbering, which is why receipt claims are keyed on store, business date and receipt number together.

### `sale_payloads`

The POS payloads of the sales of one store and business date, kept here instead of in `invoices.raw`, `invoice_lines.raw` and `invoice_payments.raw` (see the storage rule). Primary key (`store_id`, `business_date`); deleted with its store or tenant.

| Column | Type | Null | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tenant_id` | uuid | no | Tenant |
| `store_id` | uuid | no | Store |
| `business_date` | date | no | Business date of the sales, as on `invoices` |
| `payload` | text | no | One JSON record per sale, records separated by line breaks and ordered by `external_id`, compressed with lz4: `external_id` (the invoice's), `invoice` (the sale payload), `lines` (the payload of each invoice line keyed by its `external_id`) and `payments` (the payload of each tender keyed by its `sequence_no`) |
| `updated_at` | timestamptz | no | Last write |

Row level security is on with no policy, and `anon` and `authenticated` hold no privilege: only the service role reads and writes it. The sync writes it through `app.store_sale_payloads(tenant, store, business_date, records text[])` (service role only) in the transaction that writes the store-day, before the invoice rows: the call replaces the records of the given sales, keeps every other record of the day (a voided sale keeps its payload), and takes a sale whose business date changed out of the document of the date its invoice row still carries, removing a document left empty. It refuses a record without `external_id` or the same sale twice (22023).

## Consent and legal records

These tables implement the duties of the personal-data law and the anti-spam decree described in ADR-021: a per-purpose consent ledger with evidence and a working opt-out, data-subject requests tracked against their statutory deadlines, and an outbound-message log kept at least one year.

### `consent_records`

Append-only ledger of consent. One row per grant; a revocation sets `revoked_at` on the row instead of deleting it, so the evidence of both events survives. At most one active row (`revoked_at` null) exists per (`tenant_id`, `subject_type`, `subject_id`, `purpose`), enforced by the partial unique index `consent_records_one_active_per_subject_purpose_key`; a new grant after a revocation is a new row.

#### Append-only rule

The trigger `consent_records_keep_grant` (`app.keep_consent_grant()`, before update) enforces the ledger for every role, the service role included, because it is a data rule rather than an access rule. Every column except `revoked_at` is frozen once the row exists: an update that touches `tenant_id`, `subject_type`, `subject_id`, `purpose`, `policy_version`, `channel`, `granted_at`, `evidence` or `created_at` fails with `consent_records is an append-only ledger: only revoked_at may change`. `revoked_at` may go from null to a timestamp once; clearing it or moving it fails with `consent_records revocation is final`, and a value before `granted_at` fails with `consent_records revocation cannot precede the grant`. `authenticated` holds no `DELETE` privilege on the table, so a wrong grant is corrected by revoking it and recording a new one.

#### Deadline rule

`app.dsr_deadlines(received, country_code, time_zone, uses_processor)` returns the two deadlines:

- `first_response_due_at` is `received` plus two working days. A working day is Monday to Friday in `time_zone` that is not a global `holidays` row (`tenant_id` null) of `country_code`; tenant-specific custom holidays do not count. Counting starts on the day after receipt and keeps the time of day, so a request received Friday 10:00 is due Tuesday 10:00, and one received on 29 April is due 5 May because 30 April, 1 May and the weekend are skipped. The function scans the 120 days after receipt and raises an exception if it cannot find two working days in that window, so the deadline is never silently null.
- `completion_due_at` is `received` plus 20 calendar days, or 30 when `uses_processor` is true.

The trigger `data_subject_requests_fill_deadlines` runs before insert, reads the tenant's `country_code` and `timezone`, and fills whichever of the two due columns was left null. Values given explicitly are kept.

## Analytics

Roadmap phase 8 (ADR-010, ADR-026). Aggregates are ordinary tables refreshed by a function, protected by the same store-scoped RLS as the fact tables, and read by `security invoker` query functions so that RLS always applies to the caller. The metric definitions that these objects implement are in `docs/metrics.md`.

### Hot and cold rule

Every store-day with `business_date` **before yesterday** is read from the aggregate tables; **yesterday and later** are computed live from the fact tables. `app.analytics_live_from()` returns the cut-over date (`current_date - 1`, evaluated in the database's UTC clock, which always covers the local "yesterday" of a UTC+7 store). The query functions apply the rule through `app.daily_sales()` and `app.hourly_sales()`, so today is correct after every incremental sync without a refresh, and yesterday is correct even before the nightly refresh has run. The live half passes the requested `store_ids` down to `app.sales_invoices()`, which reads `invoices` through the `(store_id, business_date)` index, so a call costs the requested stores' invoices of the live days and never a scan over other tenants. Item, category, payment, shift, size, modifier, attach and basket aggregates have no live counterpart: they appear for a day once that day has been refreshed (the incremental sync refreshes the days it touched; the nightly job refreshes the trailing window).

The cut-over date is a freshness rule; the **as-of day** of month-to-date figures and forecasts is a separate, store-local rule. `app.analytics_as_of(store_ids, p_at default now())` is the earliest, over the given stores, of the store-local business date at `p_at` (store timezone and `business_day_start_minutes`) minus one day: the last complete business day. Between 00:00 and 07:00 in Vietnam the UTC date still names the local previous day, so a UTC rule would drop the completed local yesterday from month to date; the store-local rule does not, and that day is served live because it is never before the cut-over date of a UTC+7 store. With no visible store it falls back to the UTC date of `p_at` minus one.

### Refresh

`app.refresh_analytics(p_tenant uuid, p_from date, p_to date) returns bigint` is `security definer` and executable by `service_role` only. It first takes the transaction-level advisory lock `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('refresh_analytics'), hashtext(p_tenant::text))`, so overlapping refreshes of one tenant (the nightly job, an incremental sync, a manager's button) queue behind each other instead of racing on delete-then-insert, while refreshes of different tenants run in parallel. Then, in the same transaction, it logs the start, deletes every aggregate row of the tenant in the range (`agg_basket_pairs_28d` for `as_of_date = p_to`; `agg_shift_cash` also by shift, see above), re-inserts them from the fact tables, refreshes the basket pairs for `p_to`, and closes the log row with the row count. A rolled-back refresh leaves no log row. The seed calls it for the demo tenant, so `supabase db reset` leaves the aggregates populated. On the seed (two stores, seven days, about 350 invoices) a refresh takes about 30 ms; on a synthetic tenant of two stores with about 54,000 invoices over 91 days a 92-day refresh takes about 1.3 s, far under the two-minute acceptance limit of phase 8, and `month_pace` and `forecast_accuracy` answer in under 0.1 s.

`public.refresh_my_analytics(p_from date, p_to date, p_tenant uuid default null) returns bigint` is the browser-callable wrapper for owners and managers. It rejects a range longer than 92 days, rejects `p_tenant` when the caller is not an owner or manager of it, and with a null `p_tenant` refreshes every tenant where the caller holds one of those roles, in tenant id order so two such calls take the tenant locks in the same order (raising `insufficient_privilege` when there is none). Staff, viewers, publishers and `anon` cannot refresh.

### Views

`v_recent_daily_sales` and `v_recent_hourly_sales` (`security_invoker = true`) select from `app.daily_sales_facts(null, current_date - 45, current_date + 1)` and `app.hourly_sales_facts(…)`, which compute the same columns as `agg_daily_sales` and `agg_hourly_sales` directly from `invoices`, `invoice_lines`, `invoice_payments` and `payment_methods`. Because the views run as the caller, RLS on the fact tables decides what they return; `anon` cannot select from them. They are the reconciliation tool for **every store the caller can see**: a filter on `store_id` applied to the view is not pushed into the function, so each select computes the 46 days for all visible stores (and, for `service_role`, every tenant). Reports read the query functions, which take `store_ids`; the views are for the app's own checks and for comparing live figures with the aggregates.

### Publisher and aggregates

The `publisher` role reads zero rows of every aggregate table, the refresh log and the live views, exactly as it reads zero rows of the fact tables: aggregates reveal revenue, and ADR-013 wants a leaked publisher credential to read no sale. The AI data pack of phase 11 is therefore produced by an owner or manager session (or by the `fnb` CLI with the service role), and the publisher only writes the resulting plan into `strategy_reports`.

## Row-level security

Every table in `public` has RLS enabled. `anon` holds no privilege on any table. `authenticated` holds `SELECT` on every table except `pos_account_tokens`, `INSERT`, `UPDATE`, `DELETE` only on the manual-input tables (`item_costs`, `payment_fee_rates`, `item_type_roles`, `holidays`, `targets`) and `strategy_reports`, and `INSERT`, `UPDATE` without `DELETE` on the legal ledgers `consent_records` and `data_subject_requests`. Default privileges are set so that a future table created by the migration role starts read-only for `authenticated` and invisible to `anon`. The sync jobs run as `service_role`, which bypasses RLS.

### Roles

| Role | Purpose | Reads | Writes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `owner` | Tenant owner | Everything of the tenant within store scope, plus `outbound_messages`; `data_subject_requests` only when the membership is not store-limited, because the policy requires an unlimited store scope, so a store-limited owner reads zero requests | Manual inputs and plans within store scope; consent records (grant, or revoke by setting `revoked_at`); data-subject requests when the membership is not store-limited |
| `manager` | Store or tenant manager | Same as owner, including the zero-request rule for a store-limited membership | Same as owner |
| `staff` | Cashier or shift lead | Everything of the tenant within store scope, including `consent_records`; no `data_subject_requests`, no `outbound_messages` | Nothing |
| `viewer` | Read-only stakeholder | Same as staff | Nothing |
| `publisher` | Service identity used by the plan-publishing skill or routine | `tenants`, `stores`, `strategy_reports`, `holidays`, `dim_calendar` only; no aggregate table, refresh log or live view | Insert into `strategy_reports` |

`owner`, `manager`, `staff` and `viewer` are the data roles. `publisher` is deliberately outside them so that a leaked publisher credential cannot read a single sale.

### Store scope

`tenant_members.store_ids` limits a member to some stores of the tenant. A null scope means every store. Store-scoped fact tables filter on `store_id`; tenant-wide catalogue tables filter on `tenant_id`; tables whose `store_id` may be null (`item_costs`, `payment_fee_rates`, `strategy_reports`) show tenant-wide rows to every member of the tenant and store rows only to members who can see that store.

## Business date

`invoices.business_date` is assigned by the sync, never by the database, with this rule:

```
local_time     = sold_at at time zone stores.timezone
business_date  = (local_time - make_interval(mins => stores.business_day_start_minutes))::date
```

A store with `business_day_start_minutes = 300` (05:00) books a sale at 00:30 local time to the previous calendar day; with the default 0 the business date is the local calendar day. Store timezones are IANA names, so a zone with daylight saving is handled by Postgres. `invoice_payments.business_date` copies the invoice value. Shifts keep their raw timestamps; reports attribute a shift to the business date of its `opened_at` using the same rule.

## Storage rule for `raw`

Storage on the free tier is finite, and a payload stored on its own row is expensive: an iPOS line carries about a hundred fields, about 3 kB as `jsonb`, and a row under 2 kB is never compressed. Kept that way, the payloads took about two thirds of the database after the full backfill of both shops, well over the 500 MB plan. The sync therefore keeps every payload, but not on the sales rows:

- The sync writes null to `invoices.raw`, `invoice_lines.raw` and `invoice_payments.raw`. The payloads of a store-day's sales go into its `sale_payloads` document, which compresses about ten times, because the sales of a day repeat the same field names and many of the same values.
- The `invoice` part of a record is the full payload the POS returned for the sale, so any future column can be back-filled without a re-sync.
- A line or tender payload leaves out the fields whose value equals the invoice payload's (store, receipt number, timestamps, cashier, customer on iPOS). Read a field missing from it from the invoice payload; every other field is kept as returned.
- `shifts.raw`, `invoice_events.raw` and master data `raw` keep the payload of that entity as returned, on the row.

With this rule the same history fits under the 300 MB ceiling. This is a sync-side rule: the database accepts any JSON. `mapSale` drops the fields shared with the invoice (`packages/sync/test/unit/payloads.test.ts`), and the Postgres repository writes the records through `app.store_sale_payloads` (`supabase/tests/19_sale_payloads.sql`). The in-memory repository keeps the payloads on its rows. Migration `20261108062820_sale_payloads.sql` moves payloads already on the rows into `sale_payloads`; a database that held sales before it returns the space after `vacuum (full, analyze) public.invoice_lines, public.invoice_payments, public.invoices;`.

## Tenant-consistent composite foreign keys

A row that carries both `tenant_id` and a reference to a child of the tenant (a store or a POS account) references the pair, not the child's id alone:

`stores` and `pos_accounts` declare `unique (tenant_id, id)`; every store-scoped table declares `foreign key (tenant_id, store_id) references stores (tenant_id, id)` and every account-scoped table declares `foreign key (tenant_id, pos_account_id) references pos_accounts (tenant_id, id)`.

The database therefore refuses a row that names tenant A together with a store of tenant B, even when written by the service role or by a bug in a policy. Tables that follow the pattern: `stores`, every catalogue table, `store_items`, `devices`, `invoices`, `invoice_lines`, `invoice_payments`, `invoice_events`, `sale_payloads`, `shifts`, `sync_runs`, `sync_checks`, `item_costs`, `payment_fee_rates`, `item_type_roles`, `targets`, `strategy_reports`, and every `agg_*` aggregate table. `invoice_events.invoice_id` references `invoices.id` alone with `on delete set null`, because the event must outlive the invoice; its `store_id` and `invoice_external_id` keep it attributable. The legal tables carry `tenant_id` and no store; `consent_records.subject_id` and `data_subject_requests.subject_id` deliberately have no foreign key (see the consent model), and `outbound_messages.consent_record_id` references `consent_records.id` alone with `on delete set null`.
